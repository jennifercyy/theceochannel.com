There is a version of this interview that never would have happened two years ago. Back then, the questions a chief financial officer got asked in public were about growth: how fast, how much, how soon it would compound. The answers were rehearsed and the numbers were large.

That quarter is over. When Grab reported in August, the headline was not the top line — it was the deliberate decision to let it slow.

## The end of growth at any cost

“We stopped paying for demand that left the moment the incentive did,” the CFO said. “That is not a growth strategy. That is renting a number.” The shift, described here for the first time in detail, began with a single instruction from the board: report the business the way an owner would read it.

What followed was a year of unglamorous work — consolidating twelve regional forecasts into one, killing metrics that flattered without informing, and rebuilding the investor deck around three questions instead of thirty.

## What the board asked for instead

The answer, in the end, was cash. Not adjusted cash, not cash before the things that make cash hard — actual money, moving in an actual direction. “Once you agree on that as the scoreboard,” the CFO said, “most of the arguments go away.”

Whether the market rewards the discipline is another question. The early signs, six weeks on, are quietly encouraging.
